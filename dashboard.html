How discovery works

Search finds creators; channel feeds expand the discoveries. A “channel search” is a recurring check of a known creator, not a keyword search across all their uploads.

Algorithm reviewed November 4, 2026. The statistics elsewhere on this page remain the dated November 2 snapshot.

What repeats, and what limits it?

  • Channel loop: normally revisit after 6 hours if the job gained credited discoveries since its previous run, otherwise 12 hours. Empty, never-productive channels can be retired if they have no verified archive or pending candidates.
  • Keyword loop: rotate date slices; adjust revisit delays using credited yield and empty runs. Idle exploration can advance eligible slices without clearing failures or source cooldowns.
  • Pacing: search HTTP starts are at least 60 seconds apart by default, including exact-ID lookups and redirect hops. Other permitted work can continue independently; global pauses stop all work. Matching RSS requests can be shared for up to 60 seconds without changing the original observation time.
  • Coverage gap: a video that has aged out of RSS may be unverifiable even if search finds it. The catalogue supplies eligibility, not replacement numeric-zero evidence. More terms cannot guarantee full coverage or bypass access checks.

Archive retention

Clicking a video does not remove it from yt0. The archive keeps videos that qualified as zero-view discoveries, even if they later gain views.

  • The original zero-view evidence and observation time are preserved. Already verified records are not automatically rechecked or overwritten.
  • A gallery thumbnail is a normal link to YouTube. Watching may add a YouTube view; it does not trigger removal from yt0. There is no click-to-delete or watched-video eviction.
  • Filters, pagination and new discoveries can move or hide a video in the current gallery view; they do not delete its archive entry.
  • Retention has no automatic age expiry. Maintenance prunes caches and old metrics, not the verified archive.
  • yt0 saves the archive entry and evidence, not a copy of the video. A later private/deleted YouTube video can remain listed but may no longer play. Keeping an entry is not a guarantee of playback or protection against server/disk loss.

Video qualifications

New RSS-verified entries. Rules reviewed November 3, 2026.

t_pub := dateOnly(pub) ? utcDayStart(pub) + 24h : timestamp(pub)

eligible :=
    validTime(t_pub, t_obs) ∧ t_obs − t_pub ≥ 168h
  ∧ rss.statistics.@views === "0"
  ∧ validIds(video, channel) ∧ identitiesMatch(rss, metadata)
  ∧ trim(rss.title) ≠ ""
  ∧ metadata.matches(video, channel)
  ∧ isSafeInteger(duration_s) ∧ duration_s > 0
  ∧ ¬flags.any(LIVE, UPCOMING, SCHEDULED, PREMIERING)
  ∧ metadata.viewHint ≠ POSITIVE

if eligible:
    archiveOnce(video, {
        source: "youtube/rss",
        field: "media:statistics/@views",
        rawValue: "0",
        checkedAt: t_obs
    })

Refresh pre-cutoff zero observations after day seven. Unknown ≠ zero. Shorts allowed; no maximum age. Legacy imports require independent qualification. Playback is not tested.

Implementation: age and date checks, RSS and metadata verification, evidence storage and retention, gallery links.
